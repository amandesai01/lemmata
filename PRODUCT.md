# Product

## Platform

web

## Stack

Astro, chosen by the user. Content is authored as Markdown/MDX, with mathematical notation rendered at build time (KaTeX was the proposed approach). Deploy target: undecided.

## Users

**Independent learners** are the primary audience: anyone studying higher mathematics on their own who finds the site and uses its notes, problems and reading lists, whether or not they ever have contact with the group. They arrive with no access to the group's discussions, so the material has to work on the page by itself.

Group members, prospective members and academics were not chosen as primary audiences. The site is still the group's homepage, but it is not designed around them.

## Product Purpose

Lemmata is an independent mathematics study group that explores mathematics beyond the conventional undergraduate curriculum. The website is the group's academic homepage. It is a central place to discover the group's areas of study, read learning materials, explore mathematical subjects and work through problem sets.

The site documents the group's academic interests and provides the shared academic foundation for its work. Success means an independent learner can pick up a subject, follow its notes and references, and get better at mathematical reasoning and proof by working its problems.

Defining line, from the user: **Lemmata is an independent space for the study and exploration of mathematics.**

## Positioning

- Structured like a university mathematics department's site: organised by subject, with lecture-style notes, references, supplementary reading and problems. It takes the department's structure only. It has no institutional affiliation and must never imply one.
- Not an online course platform, and not trying to deliver a conventional educational experience. It is a study group's working record, published.
- Goes beyond the undergraduate curriculum and puts rigour first: proofs, hard questions and problems meant to build intuition and rigour.
- The site carries no authorship: no names, bylines or author roles. It exists for reading and practising without distractions.

## Operating Context

- Readers study a subject through its notes, follow references and supplementary reading, and work its problems.
- The group also meets regularly and informally to study and solve problems together. **The meetups are deliberately left off the public site:** no schedules, locations, sign-up or invitations to join.

## Capabilities and Constraints

- **Subjects:** discrete mathematics, abstract algebra, group theory, number theory, Galois theory and other areas of higher mathematics. The list will grow.
- **Per subject:** lecture-style notes, references, supplementary reading and problems.
- **Authorship:** never shown. No author names, bylines or author fields anywhere on the site.
- **Status:** never shown. Nothing is labelled in progress, planned or placeholder, and nothing is labelled complete either.
- **Mathematical notation** is central and must render correctly across the site.
- **Out of scope unless decided later:** course-platform mechanics such as enrolment, accounts, progress tracking, certificates and grading.
- **Undecided:** deploy target, how and whether membership or joining is described, licensing of the notes, and the URL/domain.

## Brand Commitments

- Name: **Lemmata**.
- Defining line: "Lemmata is an independent space for the study and exploration of mathematics."
- Voice: academic and serious about mathematics. The content is about studying seriously, asking difficult questions and constructing proofs.
- Structural reference: a university mathematics department's pages, as stated by the user. This covers structure only, not a visual reference and not an affiliation.
- No logo or other identity assets exist yet.

## Evidence on Hand

None yet. The project folder is empty and no notes have been written. The site will launch with subject outlines, and notes will be added over time.

Future work must not fabricate:
- lecture notes, problem sets, solutions or reading lists presented as the group's real material;
- member names, authors, bios or credentials;
- meetup details;
- university or institutional affiliations, endorsements or testimonials.

Where content is missing, leave it off the site rather than marking it as a placeholder.

## Product Principles

1. **Written for the reader who isn't in the room.** The main reader never attends a meetup. Notes, problems and reading lists must make sense without the discussion that produced them.
2. **A department, not a course.** Organise by subject and material, the way a mathematics department does. No funnels, streaks, enrolment or sales pitch for learning.
3. **Rigour is the point.** The material goes past the standard curriculum and is about proof. Don't water it down to widen its appeal.
4. **No authors, no distractions.** No names, bylines or author roles anywhere. The reader meets the mathematics and nothing else.
5. **Complete within itself.** Every page is presented as complete. Nothing is marked in progress, planned or placeholder, and nothing is labelled complete; content carries no status at all. What isn't ready is left off the site.
